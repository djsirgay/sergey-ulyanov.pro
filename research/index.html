Research direction · 2026

Who gets to teach AI what a culture means?

I am developing a practice-led research agenda on community-governed AI for Belarusian language learning and cultural continuity among adults in exile. The aim is not a synthetic national personality. It is a source-visible learning environment that keeps authority, disagreement, and correction with the community.

Question
Can AI expand language practice without flattening identity or accelerating Russification?
Method
Participatory design · model audit · mixed methods · design-based research
Context
Belarusian adults, artists, and cultural workers living across borders
Status
Protocol and pilot materials prepared; no human-subject findings claimed
Primary research program

Community-governed AI for cultural continuity.

The working question is intentionally bounded: how can a culturally grounded, community-accountable AI system support heritage-language learning and participation among geographically dispersed adults without replacing teachers, artists, or human cultural authority?

Design principle 01

Provenance before authority.

Language and cultural claims should show where they came from, what remains uncertain, and who has the right to correct, annotate, or remove them.

Design principle 02

Many voices, not one synthetic Belarusian.

Regional variation, political disagreement, queer experience, migration, and generational difference are part of the knowledge — not noise to be averaged away.

Design principle 03

Learning by making.

Conversation practice can be paired with creative projects: documenting a story, annotating a song, preparing a release, or contributing context under revocable consent.

Methods bridge

A pilot designed to be audited, not admired.

The first phase is a bounded non-human-subject audit of existing AI systems. It tests the evaluation instrument before any community recruitment and creates a concrete artifact for methodological critique.

36prompts across language, culture, provenance, uncertainty, and political bias
3AI systems compared with identical prompts and frozen response records
2+independent Belarusian-speaking raters before any reliability claim
1transparent codebook for naturalness, specificity, sourcing, and harm
01Freeze prompts

Record wording, model, date, settings, and output without post-hoc editing.

02Blind the ratings

Remove system identity before independent scoring and qualitative notes.

03Compare judgments

Calculate agreement and inspect disagreements instead of hiding them.

04Revise after critique

Document what changed after academic and community methodological review.

Research boundary: the workbook, prompt bank, codebook, and scoring template are prepared. Results will not be presented until responses are frozen, a second qualified rater participates, and the analysis is reviewed for overclaiming.

Practice as evidence

The research question did not appear from nowhere.

These projects establish lived access, cultural practice, systems-building experience, and testable hypotheses. They do not substitute for scholarly methods or peer review.
