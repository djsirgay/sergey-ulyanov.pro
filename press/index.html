Short bio · select and copy for introductions

Sergéy Ulyanov is a Los Angeles-based creative and marketing strategist, musician, actor, and founder of NostalgAI Recordz, an independent label supporting musicians in exile. Originally from Belarus, he brings 7.5 years of LGBTQ+ communications and community outreach to his work on identity, belonging, and rebuilding a creative life. His experience spans public-health materials, practical information for newcomers on YouTube, music production, and independent research on Belarusian-language cultural memory. He connects creative entrepreneurship with responsible uses of AI, while keeping human judgment and artists’ rights central. He speaks in English, Belarusian, and Russian, offering firsthand perspectives on displacement, cultural continuity, and creating opportunities for others across borders.

The longer story: displacement, support, and rebuilding

Community communication. Through his volunteer work with Vstrecha in Belarus, Sergéy helped create public-health booklets, posters, and websites and supported community outreach around prevention and access to testing. In the United States, he has used YouTube to share practical information with newcomers and helped people look for potential sponsors.

Public reporting on his outreach and newcomer information: SlavicSac · 2024 · Rubic · 2023

Music as archival work. In an August 2026 interview, Sergéy described recovering recordings for the RAY anniversary edition from collaborators, old correspondence, and scattered digital copies after losing his archive. The project restores song histories and credits for more than 80 contributors in its booklets, connecting his own creative history with the people who helped make it.

Source: SlavicSac · August 17, 2026

Displacement and support. After fleeing persecution in Belarus and the war in Ukraine, Sergéy reached the United States via Mexico, spent nearly a month in detention, and was granted asylum in 2024. Human Rights First helped connect him with pro bono counsel at Cooley LLP—an experience that now informs his public storytelling about access to support, belonging, and rebuilding a creative life.

Sources: Nasha Niva · Human Rights First
